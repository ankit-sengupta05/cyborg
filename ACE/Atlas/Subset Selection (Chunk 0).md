Subset Selection •There are two reasons why we are often not satisfied with the least squares estimates . •Prediction accuracy •Interpretation •Subset selection in regression is a technique used to identify a subset of predictor variables (features) that provide the best model for predicting the response variable . •This is especially useful in situations where you have a large number of predictors , but you want to avoid overfitting or include only the most significant variables in your model . Subset Selection There are several different strategies for choosing the subset . •Best Subset Selection •Forward Stepwise Selection . •Backward Stepwise Selection •Stepwise Selection (Hybrid Method) Subset Selection 1. Best Subset Selection •Best subset regression finds for each k ∈ {0, 1, 2, . . . , p} the subset of size k that gives the smallest residual sum of squares . •For a given set of 𝑝 predictors , Best Subset Selection involves fitting regression models for every possible combination of these predictors . •This means fitting 2𝑝 models, ranging from the model with no predictors to the full model with all 𝑝 predictors . Subset Selection Example of Best Subset Selection •Step 1: Suppose we have a dataset with p = 3 predictor variables and one response variable, y. To perform the best subset selection with this dataset, we would fit the following 2p = 23 = 8 models . •A model with no predictors •A model with predictor x1 •A model with predictor x2 •A model with predictor x3 •A model with predictors x1, x2 •A model with predictors x1, x3 •A model with predictors x2, x3 •A model with predictors x1, x2, x3 •Step 2: Next, we’d choose the model with the highest R2 among each set of models with k predictors . •A model with no predictors •A model with predictor x2 •A model with predictors x1, x2 •A model with predictors x1, x2, x3For example, we might end up choosing Subset Selection Example of Best Subset Selection •Step 3: perform cross -validation and choose the best model to be the one that results in the lowest prediction error . •Step 4: Criteria for Choosing the “Best” Model The last step of best subset selection involves choosing the model with •The lowest prediction error, •lowest Cp, •lowest BIC ( Bayesian information criteria) , •lowest AIC( Akaike’s Information Criteria) , •or highest adjusted R2. Subset Selection Pros & Cons of Best Subset Selection pros •It’s a straightforward approach to understand and interpret . •It allows us to identify the best possible model since we consider all combinations of predictor variables . cons •It can be computationally intense . For a set of p predictor variables, there are 2p possible models . For example, with 10 predictor variables there are 210 = 1,000 possible models to consider . •Because it considers such a large number of models , it could potentially find a model that performs well on training data but not on future data. This could result in overfitting . Subset Selection – 2. Stepwise Selection Forward Stepwise Selection •Begins with a model that contains no variables (called the Null Model) •Then starts adding the most significant variables one after the other •Until a pre-specified stopping rule is reached or until all the variables under consideration are included in the model . Choose a stopping rule •If added to the model, the stopping rule is satisfied when all remaining variables to consider have a p- value larger than some specified threshold . How to determine the threshold? •A fixed value (for instance: 0.05 or 0.2 or 0.5) •Determined by AIC (Akaike Information Criterion) •Determined by BIC (Bayesian information criterion) Subset Selection – 2. Stepwise Selection Backward stepwise •Begins with a model that contains all variables under consideration (called the Full Model) •Then starts removing the least significant variables one after the other •Until a pre-specified stopping rule is reached or until no variable is left in the model Subset Selection – 2. Stepwise Selection Where forward stepwise is better •Forward stepwise selection can used when the number of variables under consideration is very large, even larger than the sample size. Where backward stepwise is better •Starting with the full model has the advantage of considering the effects of all variables simultaneously . •This is especially important in case of collinearity (when variables in a model are correlated which each other) because backward stepwise may be forced to keep them all in the model unlike forward selection where none of them might be entered . Advantages and limitations of stepwise selection •It is more computationally efficient than best subset selection . Given p predictor variables , the best subset selection must fit 2p models . •Conversely, stepwise selection only has to fit 1+p(p+ 1)/2 models .
