Consequently when testing a hypothesis with small samples, we must assume that the samples come from a normally or approximately normally distributed population. Under these conditions, the sampling distribution of sample statistic such as x and p is normal but the critical values of x or p depend on whether or not the population standard deviation σ is known. When the value of the population standard deviation σ is not known, its value is estimated by computing the standard deviation of sample s and the standard error of the mean is calculated by using the formula, xσ=/sn. When we do this, the resulting sampling distribution may not be normal even if sampling is done from a normally distributed population.
