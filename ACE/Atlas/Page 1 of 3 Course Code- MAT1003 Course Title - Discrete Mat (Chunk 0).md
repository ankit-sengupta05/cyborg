Page 1 of 3 Course Code: MAT1003 Course Title : Discrete Mathematical Structures T P C 4 0 4 Version No. 3.1 Course Pre-requisites None Anti -requisites (if any). MAT1007 Objectives: 1. To understand the notion of mathematical thinking and mathematical proofs, and to apply them in problem solving. 2. To prove and disprove mathematical statements using a variety of techniques. 3. To learn number theory, modular arithmetic and appreciate its role in cryptography and computer science. 4. To perform combinatorial analysis, not on applying formulae, to solve counting problems and analyse algorithms. 5. To address the challenges of the relevance of algebraic structures in computer science and engineering 6. To understand the concepts of graph theory and related concepts of algorithms 7. To perform scientific communication in the language of mathematics precisely without any ambiguity. CO's Mapping with PO's Course Outcomes Course Outcome Statement Program Outcomes CO1 Use logical notation to define and reason about fundamental mathematical concepts; Evaluate elementary mathematical arguments and identify fallacious reasoning; and conceive and/or analyze basic mathematical proofs. PO1, PO2 CO2 Apply the knowledge and skills obtained to investigate and solve a variety of discrete mathematical problems. PO1, PO2 CO3 Understand the basics of number theory and be able to compute with modular arithmetic and understand its use in cryptography. PO1, PO2 CO4 Effectively use algebraic techniques to analyze basic discrete structures PO1, PO2 CO5 Identify combinatorial structures and apply basic techniques in combinatorics and counting to solve problems in the context of Mathematics, Data science and Computer Science PO1, PO4 CO6 Demonstrate an understanding of how concepts of graphs and trees are used to design and solve model problems arising in data science and/or Computer Science. PO1, PO2, PO3, PO4 Total hours of Instruction: 60 Module No. 1 Logic and Proofs 14 Hours Review of Basic Structures: sets, functions. Introduction to (Propositional) Logic, Propositional Logic and Equivalences, Normal Forms, Predicates and Quantifiers, Nested Quantifiers, Rules of Inference, Introduction to Proofs, Proof Methods and Strategies Module No. 2 Number Theory 8 Hours Divisibility and modular arithmetic, Integer representations, Primes, and the greatest common divisor, the Euclidean algorithm, extended Euclidean algorithm. Solving Congruences: Linear Congruences, Chinese Remainder Theorem, Fermat’s little theorem, Primitive Roots and Discrete Logarithms. Module No. 3 Introductory Counting 9 Hours The basics of counting, The Pigeonhole principle, Binomial coefficients and identities, Basic and generalized permutations and combinations, recurrence relations (Only modelling and related problems) , generating functions, Inclusion –Exclusion. Page 2 of 3 Module No. 4 Algebraic Structures 8 Hours Groups and Subgroups, Cosets and Lagrange’s Theorem, Homomorphism, Rings, Fields – Properties. Module No. 5 Relations 7 Hours Binary and n-ary relations, Closures of Relations, Representing Relations, Equivalence Relations, Partial Orderings: Hesse diagram, Lattices and their Properties. Module No. 6 Graphs 14 Hours Graphs and Graph Models, Graph Terminology and Special Types of Graphs , Bipartite Graphs and Matchings, New Graphs from Old, Representing Graphs and Graph Isomorphism, Connectivity, Euler and Hamilton Paths, Guan’s Postman Problem , Planar Graphs , Crossing numbers , Graph Colouring: independence number, Clique number and Chromatic number, Applications of Graph Colorings.
